# Compensation rules: amounts, notice periods and delays

Topic: how much compensation is owed and when (Regulation (EC) No 261/2004, Articles 5 and 7; Sturgeon and Nelson rulings). The amount itself is calculated by code; this document explains the rules behind it.

## Amounts (Article 7(1))
- Flights of 1,500 km or less: EUR 250
- Flights within the EU over 1,500 km, and other flights between 1,500 and 3,500 km: EUR 400
- All other flights (over 3,500 km, outside the EU): EUR 600

## 50% reduction (Article 7(2))
If the passenger is offered re-routing and arrives no more than 2 hours (up to 1,500 km), 3 hours (middle band) or 4 hours (longest flights) after the originally scheduled arrival, the airline may reduce the compensation by half.

## Cancellations and notice (Article 5(1)(c))
No compensation is owed for a cancellation if the passenger was informed:
- at least two weeks before the scheduled departure; or
- between two weeks and seven days before, and offered re-routing departing no more than 2 hours before the original departure and arriving less than 4 hours after the original arrival; or
- less than seven days before, and offered re-routing departing no more than 1 hour before the original departure and arriving less than 2 hours after the original arrival.

These notice rules apply whatever the cause. The extraordinary circumstances exemption (Article 5(3)) is a separate, additional ground for refusing compensation.

## Long delays (Sturgeon and Nelson)
The regulation's text only gives compensation for cancellations. In Sturgeon (joined cases C-402/07 and C-432/07, 19 November 2009) the Court of Justice held that passengers whose flight is delayed must be treated like passengers whose flight is cancelled when they reach their final destination three hours or more after the scheduled arrival time: they are entitled to the same compensation, unless the delay was caused by extraordinary circumstances. The Court confirmed this in Nelson (joined cases C-581/10 and C-629/10, 23 October 2012).

What counts is the delay at ARRIVAL, not at departure.

Sources: Regulation (EC) No 261/2004, Articles 5 and 7; CJEU, Sturgeon and Others, C-402/07 and C-432/07, 19 November 2009; CJEU, Nelson and Others, C-581/10 and C-629/10, 23 October 2012.
