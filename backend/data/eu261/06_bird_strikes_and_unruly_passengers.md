# Bird strikes, unruly passengers and events on a previous flight

Topic: external events that hit the aircraft, and events that happened on an earlier flight of the same aircraft.

## Bird strikes: extraordinary
Covers: a bird strike, the plane hitting a bird or a goose, a collision with an animal, a mandatory inspection after a bird strike.

In Pešková and Peška v Travel Service (C-315/15, 4 May 2017), an aircraft collided with a bird while landing on the flight before the passenger's flight, and had to be checked. The Court of Justice held that a collision between an aircraft and a bird, and any damage it causes, is an extraordinary circumstance: it is not intrinsically linked to the operation of the aircraft, so it is not inherent in the normal exercise of the airline's activity, and it is beyond the airline's actual control. (The Advocate General had argued the opposite, but the Court did not follow him.)

However, the airline only benefits from the exemption for the part of the delay that was truly unavoidable. In that case, after an expert authorised to do so had already found the aircraft fit to fly, the airline sent its own technician for a second check. The Court held that this second check was not necessary, so the delay it caused could not be blamed on the bird strike. The national court has to work out whether the delay would still have reached three hours without the unjustified part.

## Unruly passengers: can be extraordinary
Covers: a violent, drunk or disruptive passenger, a diversion to remove a passenger.

In Transportes Aéreos Portugueses (C-74/19, 11 June 2020), a passenger bit another passenger and assaulted others and the crew, forcing the aircraft to divert. The Court held that the unruly behaviour of a passenger which leads to a diversion can be an extraordinary circumstance, as a security risk outside the airline's control. But not if the airline contributed to it, for example by letting a passenger board who was already behaving in a disruptive way before or during boarding.

## Events on a previous flight of the same aircraft
Covers: the incoming or inbound aircraft arriving late, a knock-on delay, a problem on the plane's earlier flight.

In the same ruling (C-74/19), the Court held that an airline may rely on an extraordinary circumstance that affected an EARLIER flight operated with the same aircraft, provided there is a direct causal link between that circumstance and the delay or cancellation of the later flight.

The reasonable-measures requirement also applies: the airline must re-route the passenger at the earliest opportunity, including on other direct or indirect flights, possibly operated by other airlines, if they arrive earlier than its own next flight.

## How to apply it
- Bird strike requiring a mandatory inspection: extraordinary (unless the delay was made longer by unnecessary steps).
- Delay caused by an event on the aircraft's previous flight: look at THAT event. If it was extraordinary (bird strike, unruly passenger, storm), the exemption can apply. If it was an ordinary technical problem or inspection, it is NOT extraordinary: the knock-on effect does not change the nature of the original cause.

Sources: CJEU, Pešková and Peška v Travel Service, C-315/15, 4 May 2017; CJEU, Transportes Aéreos Portugueses, C-74/19, 11 June 2020.
