# Extraordinary circumstances: the general test

Topic: when an airline is released from paying compensation (Regulation (EC) No 261/2004, Article 5(3) and recitals 14 and 15).

## The rule
Under Article 5(3), an airline does not have to pay compensation for a cancellation (or, following the Sturgeon ruling, for an arrival delay of three hours or more) if it can prove that the disruption was caused by extraordinary circumstances which could not have been avoided even if all reasonable measures had been taken.

Care (meals, hotel, transport, communications) is NOT affected by this exemption: it is owed even when the cause is extraordinary. The exemption only concerns compensation.

## Examples given by the regulation itself
Recital 14 lists situations where extraordinary circumstances may occur: political instability, meteorological conditions incompatible with the operation of the flight, security risks, unexpected flight safety shortcomings, and strikes that affect the operation of the airline.

Recital 15 adds that extraordinary circumstances should be deemed to exist where an air traffic management decision about a particular aircraft on a particular day causes a long delay, an overnight delay or the cancellation of flights, even though the airline took all reasonable measures to avoid it.

## The two-part test used by the Court of Justice
Since Wallentin-Hermann (C-549/07, 2008), the Court of Justice of the EU applies two cumulative conditions. An event is an extraordinary circumstance only if:
1. by its nature or origin, it is NOT inherent in the normal exercise of the airline's activity, AND
2. it is beyond the airline's actual control.

Both conditions must be met. An event that is unusual but still part of running an airline (for example most technical faults, or industrial action by the airline's own staff) fails the first condition.

## Reasonable measures
Even when the event itself is extraordinary, the airline must also show it took all reasonable measures to avoid the cancellation or delay, or to limit its consequences. If part of the delay was caused by the airline's own choices (for example an unnecessary extra check), that part does not benefit from the exemption.

## Burden of proof
The airline must prove both the extraordinary circumstance and the reasonable measures. When in doubt, the exemption does not apply.

Sources: Regulation (EC) No 261/2004, Article 5(3), recitals 14 and 15; CJEU, Wallentin-Hermann v Alitalia, C-549/07, 22 December 2008.
