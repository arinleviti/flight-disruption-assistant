# Weather and air traffic management decisions

Topic: whether bad weather or restrictions imposed by air traffic control are extraordinary circumstances.

## Weather
Covers: storms, thunderstorms, snow, ice, fog, high winds, volcanic ash, an airport closed because of the weather.

Recital 14 of the regulation names meteorological conditions incompatible with the operation of the flight as a situation where extraordinary circumstances may occur. Severe weather such as thunderstorms, heavy snow, fog that closes an airport or volcanic ash is outside the airline's activity and beyond its control.

What matters is that the weather actually prevented the flight from operating safely (for example, the airport was closed or departures were suspended). Ordinary weather that the airline should plan for does not qualify on its own.

## Air traffic management decisions
Covers: air traffic control restrictions, departures suspended or reduced by the authorities, slot restrictions, airspace closures.

Recital 15 says extraordinary circumstances should be deemed to exist where an air traffic management decision concerning a particular aircraft on a particular day causes a long delay, an overnight delay or the cancellation of flights, provided the airline took all reasonable measures to avoid it. Examples: air traffic control suspending or restricting departures, slot restrictions imposed because of capacity problems, airspace closures decided by the authorities.

These decisions are taken by third parties (air traffic control, the authorities) and are beyond the airline's control.

## Reasonable measures still apply
Even when the weather or an air traffic control decision is the cause, the airline must show it did what it reasonably could, for example re-routing passengers promptly. If the airline's own choices made the disruption longer, that part is not covered.

## How to apply it
- Storms, airport closures, departures suspended by air traffic control: generally extraordinary.
- Care (meals, hotel) is still owed in these cases; only compensation is excluded.

Sources: Regulation (EC) No 261/2004, recitals 14 and 15, Article 5(3).
